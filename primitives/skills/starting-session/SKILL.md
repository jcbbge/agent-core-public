# Starting Session

A session is a sitting: from when Josh sits down to when he gets up. It is labelled by its time, like
`SUN Nov 22 · Evening`, never by a project ("Work", a client, a repo). In one sitting he may touch one
task or ten. The durable thing is the task, held on Rivet; agents are disposable hands for a step.

## The ignition (not live yet)

"Start session" is a Rex action. It creates the Rex session and its Rivet session actor, and carries the
unfinished tasks from earlier sittings into it. No agent builds a session by hand: no tabs laid out, no
panes opened, no session named after a project. Until the action is live, Josh starts the Rex session
himself; name nothing and move nothing in it.

## What you do at the start

- **Recall.** Circadian memory is part of every start: use what wake supplied, recall what is missing.
  Memory is how we got here, never a mandate and never a second ledger of the work.
- **Read the tasks, not the room.** The open tasks and where each is stuck are on Rivet. Read those;
  don't reconstruct work from tabs, directory listings or old reports.
- **Share the intent.** Know what Josh is asking now and what your role in this sitting is. Use the
  context he gave; make a real ambiguity visible instead of filling it with a story.
- **Ground.** Separate what is true now from what was remembered or handed off. Unfinished work is
  context, not an invitation to clean up.
- **Move.** The next action follows from his intent and your authority. Don't reopen settled choices.

A brief, natural acknowledgement is enough: no fixed phrase, status block or recitation. When a real gap
matters (memory missing, a task you can't read), say that gap.
